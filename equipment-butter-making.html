Continuous Butter Making Machine

03 • NUMBERED CONSTRUCTION

Eight functional sections

Each section has a specific role in converting cream into finished butter. Together they provide controlled churning, separation, working, dosing, air removal and final product adjustment.

1

Churning cylinder

A double-cooled horizontal cylinder containing rapidly rotating beaters. Prepared cream enters continuously and is subjected to intense mechanical agitation. The very short residence time is sufficient to destabilise the fat emulsion and form butter grains while leaving the process continuous.

2

Separation section

The newly formed butter grains and buttermilk move into the separation section, which also begins the first working action. Buttermilk is removed continuously while the butter is conveyed forward by a screw. Chilled recirculated buttermilk can be used during this stage to wash and cool the butter grains.

3

Squeeze-drying section

Butter passes through a conical channel and perforated plate where mechanical squeezing removes residual buttermilk. This stage reduces free liquid between the grains and prepares the butter for more controlled working.

4

Second working section

The butter is worked further to develop a continuous fat phase and a fine dispersion of water. The working intensity can be controlled independently; the first working screw is normally operated faster than the following working screw to obtain the required structure.

5

Injection section

An injection chamber allows controlled addition of materials such as salt solution after the principal working stage. Accurate dosing and rapid subsequent working help distribute the added material uniformly through the butter.

6

Vacuum working section

The butter passes through a section connected to a vacuum system. Reduced pressure removes entrained air and helps bring the air content closer to that of conventionally churned butter. This also contributes to the characteristic body and handling properties of the finished product.

7

Final working stage

The final stage contains several small working chambers separated by perforated plates. Different perforation sizes and impeller geometries intensify and regulate the final working, producing a uniform texture and finely dispersed aqueous phase.

8

Moisture control unit

Online measurement can monitor moisture, salt, density and temperature at the outlet. Moisture adjustment can be made during the final working stage, allowing the process to maintain a consistent target when cream characteristics remain stable.

04 • CHURNING

1 — Churning cylinder

The churning cylinder is the point where the cream emulsion is rapidly destabilised and butter grains are formed.

Feed condition

The machine receives prepared cream from the preceding cream-treatment and ripening system. Cream composition and temperature influence the required churning and working conditions.

Mechanical action

Rapidly rotating beaters create intense agitation. The fat globule membranes are disrupted and fat coalesces into visible butter grains while the remaining aqueous phase becomes buttermilk.

Why speed matters

Beater speed and product flow must be coordinated. The objective is to form grains of suitable size for efficient buttermilk drainage and subsequent working. Excessive or insufficient mechanical action can disturb the desired grain formation and separation behaviour.

Key idea

Fast churning creates butter grains; controlled downstream working turns those grains into a uniform butter mass.

05 • SEPARATION & DRYING

2–3 — Removing buttermilk and free liquid

Separation section

Butter grains and buttermilk leave the churning cylinder together. In the separation section, buttermilk is continuously drained while a screw conveys and begins working the butter. Chilled recirculated buttermilk may contact the grains during this stage.

The screw action begins consolidation of the butter and helps transport it toward the next section without interrupting the continuous process.

Squeeze-drying section

After separation, the butter passes through a conical channel and perforated plate. Mechanical compression removes remaining buttermilk and free liquid from between the butter grains.

This step prepares the butter for the more intensive working stages, where the desired continuous fat phase and fine water dispersion are developed.

06 • WORKING

4 — Second working section

Working is not simply mixing: it changes the physical structure of the butter by pressing and deforming the grains so that the fat becomes a continuous phase and moisture becomes finely dispersed.

Consolidation

Butter grains are pressed together and gradually form a coherent butter mass.

Water dispersion

Moisture is distributed as very fine droplets within the continuous fat phase. A properly worked butter should not show visible free water.

Independent working speeds

Separate working sections can operate at different speeds. The earlier working screw is normally faster than the following screw, allowing progressive rather than uncontrolled treatment.

07 • INJECTION & VACUUM

5–6 — Dosing and vacuum working

Injection section

Following the main working stages, a controlled injection system can introduce salt in a suitable form. Accurate dosing is important because the added material must be distributed rapidly and uniformly during the subsequent working.

Injection can also be integrated with final product adjustment depending on the machine arrangement and formulation requirement.

Vacuum working section

The vacuum section is connected to a vacuum system. Reduced pressure removes entrained air from the butter. This allows the air content to be brought closer to conventionally churned butter and can influence body, density and spreadability.

Vacuum is a finishing treatment

It removes entrained air from the butter mass; it is not the step responsible for forming the initial butter grains.

09 • FINAL WORKING

7 — Final working stage

The final working stage refines the structure established earlier in the machine. It is divided into several small chambers separated by perforated plates.

A

Perforated plates

Different perforation sizes control how the butter passes between working chambers and influence the intensity of mechanical treatment.

B

Working impellers

Different impeller geometries provide controlled deformation and mixing as the butter moves through the finishing train.

C

Final moisture adjustment

The first small final-working section can incorporate an injector for final adjustment of the moisture level.

D

Texture development

Repeated controlled working helps produce a smooth, coherent butter mass with finely dispersed moisture.

10 • MOISTURE CONTROL

8 — Online product control

Continuous manufacture benefits from monitoring product properties at the outlet. The control section can use online signals to maintain consistent product quality.

MoistureMonitors water content and supports final adjustment.
SaltMonitors salt content where salted butter is produced.
DensityProvides an additional indication of product condition.
TemperatureTracks the thermal condition of the finished butter stream.

Control principle

When cream characteristics remain stable, continuous measurement and controlled adjustment can keep the final moisture level very close to its target. This is one of the major advantages of integrating process measurement with the buttermaker.

11 • PRODUCT FORMATION

From butter grains to a continuous butter phase

During churning, the fat globules of cream are destabilised and coalesce into butter grains. The aqueous phase remains as buttermilk and is removed continuously rather than by stopping the machine and draining a batch.

After drainage, working presses and squeezes the butter grains. Liquid fat and fat crystals contribute to the development of a continuous fat phase, while the remaining water is broken up and dispersed finely throughout the butter.

The finished structure should be coherent and smooth, with moisture distributed sufficiently finely that visible water droplets are absent. Further working, vacuum treatment, dosing and final adjustment refine this structure before the butter leaves the machine.

13 • OPERATING CAPABILITY

Continuous production

Production capacity

Continuous buttermaking machines are designed for industrial production and can operate at substantially higher throughput than small batch churns. Typical machine capacities vary with design, product and configuration.

Direct downstream handling

The finished butter leaves the end nozzle as a continuous ribbon and can be conveyed to a butter silo and then onward to packaging, reducing repeated manual handling between processing stages.

14 • ADVANTAGES

Why continuous buttermaking is used

Continuous operation

Cream moves continuously from the prepared/ripened cream supply through churning, separation, drying, working and final adjustment instead of being handled as separate batches.

Rapid butter formation

High-speed mechanical agitation in the churning cylinder rapidly destabilises the cream emulsion and produces butter grains in a very short residence time.

Continuous buttermilk removal

The butter grains and buttermilk are separated continuously, allowing the process to move directly into controlled working and moisture reduction.

Fine water dispersion

Working converts the butter grains into a continuous fat phase in which the aqueous phase is finely dispersed, which is essential for a dry-looking, smooth butter structure.

Closed hygienic process

The integrated machine can operate as a closed process, reducing unnecessary product exposure and manual handling.

Online control

Moisture, salt, density and temperature can be monitored at the outlet, allowing process conditions to be adjusted as needed.

16 • PROCESS DATA

Important temperatures, composition and operating values

Butter manufacture is strongly dependent on cream fat crystallisation and the temperature at which churning begins. The following values are useful engineering ranges and examples; the actual set-points must be selected for the cream's fat composition, iodine value, ripening history, machine design and product specification.

Temperature is a texture control

Milk fat is not a single uniform fat. Its solid/liquid balance changes with temperature and composition. Therefore, the same churning temperature should not automatically be applied to every cream lot.

Do not chase one universal set-point

Research on continuous churning shows that cream feed temperature, cream flow rate, fat content, physical ripening time and beater shear all influence the resulting butter water content.

Moisture is a critical control variable

Water must be finely dispersed in the continuous fat phase. Excess free moisture, poor distribution or incorrect final moisture adjustment can affect texture, appearance and compliance.

17 • ENGINEERING SUMMARY

Remember the machine as one continuous transformation

Formation side

Churning → separation → squeeze-drying forms the butter grains, removes buttermilk and begins consolidation.

Finishing side

Working → injection → vacuum → final working → moisture control develops the final butter structure and adjusts its composition and air content.
