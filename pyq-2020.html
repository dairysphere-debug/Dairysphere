PREVIOUS YEAR QUESTIONS • 2020

2020 Dairy Technology PYQ

120 questions • Answers and explanations in webpage text format.

Q1
Bactofugation is a process which is used to separate
(1) Microorganisms from milk
(2) Fat from milk
(3) Whey proteins in milk
(4) Minerals from milk
Answer: (1) Microorganisms from milk
Explanation
Bactofugation uses a specially designed high-speed centrifuge to
selectively separate and remove dense bacterial spores and vegetative cells from
milk.
Q2
Lyophilization is the technical term for
(1) Vacuum drying
(2) Freeze drying
(3) Drum drying
(4) Osmotic dehydration
Answer: (2) Freeze drying
Explanation
Lyophilization is a process where the product is frozen, and pressure is
reduced to allow ice to sublimates directly to vapor.
Q3
Sterilization temperature-time employed for evaporated milk is
(1) 95°C to 110°C for 15 min.
(2) 116°C to 118°C for 15 min.
(3) 120°C to 125°C for 15 min.
(4) 128°C to 132°C for 15 min.
Answer: (2) 116°C to 118°C for 15 min.
Explanation
Standard in-can batch sterilization for evaporated milk generally
requires a temperature of 116°C to 118°C held for about 15 minutes.
Q4
The colour of buffalo ghee is greenish yellow due to the presence of
(1) Bilirubin and Biliverdin
(2) Carotene
(3) Xanthophyll
(4) Chlorophyll
Answer: (1) Bilirubin and Biliverdin
Explanation
Unlike cow ghee which is yellow due to carotene, buffalo ghee is
whitish but can possess a greenish-yellow tinge due to the bile pigments bilirubin and
biliverdin.
Q5
The destabilization of cream in hot coffee is commonly known as
(1) Coagulation of cream
(2) Feathering of cream
(3) Clotting of cream
(4) Denaturation of cream
Answer: (2) Feathering of cream
Explanation
High heat, combined with the low pH and tannins in coffee, causes
casein proteins in cream to instantly coagulate into unsightly white flakes, termed
'feathering'.
Q6
Which one of the following is a coagulating enzyme?
(1) Lactase
(2) Lipase
(3) Beta-galactosidase
(4) Chymosin
Answer: (4) Chymosin
Explanation
Chymosin (the primary active enzyme in rennet) specifically cleaves
the kappa-casein chain, destabilizing the micelles and causing milk to coagulate into a
curd.
Q7
According to FSSAI, the full cream sweetened condensed milk should
contain not less than
(1) 21% milk solids
(2) 25% milk solids
(3) 28% milk solids
(4) 31% milk solids
Answer: (4) 31% milk solids
Explanation
FSSAI standards mandate that full cream sweetened condensed milk
must contain a minimum of 9% milk fat and not less than 31% total milk solids.
Q8
The fat content in plastic cream is in the range of
(1) 40-50%
(2) 51-55%
(3) 55-60%
(4) 65-80%
Answer: (4) 65-80%
Explanation
Plastic cream is an ultra-heavy cream that is mechanically separated to
contain 65% to 85% fat, exhibiting a solid, plastic-like consistency at room
temperature.
Q9
The cheese having a soft creamy interior with a typical pungent flavor
and surface-ripened principally by a mold
(1) Camembert
(2) Ricotta
(3) Emmentaler
(4) Romano
Answer: (1) Camembert
Explanation
Camembert (and Brie) are classic soft-ripened cheeses characterized by
a creamy interior and a bloomy, surface-ripened white rind formed primarily by the
mold Penicillium camemberti.
Q10
FFA content of ghee is expressed as
(1) % palmitic acid
(2) % oleic acid
(3) % stearic acid
(4) % butyric acid
Answer: (2) % oleic acid
Explanation
Free Fatty Acid (FFA) content in fats and oils is standardly titrated
against a base and expressed chemically as a percentage of Oleic acid by mass.
Q11
Which of the following is NOT the cause of sandiness defect in ice
cream?
(1) High MSNF / lactose content
(2) High fat content
(3) Temperature fluctuation during storage
(4) Long storage period
Answer: (2) High fat content
Explanation
Sandiness is strictly caused by the massive crystallization of lactose.
High fat content actually creates a smoother texture. The other three directly cause or
promote lactose crystal growth.
Q12
Which one of the following milk products has several layers of clotted
cream?
(1) Burfi
(2) Kalakand
(3) Rabri
(4) Kheer
Answer: (3) Rabri
Explanation
Rabri is prepared by slowly heating milk and continuously skimming
off the clotted cream (malai) skin that forms on top, resulting in a multi-layered
texture.
Q13
Which of the following takes place during vacreation of cream?
(1) Removal of undesirable odours
(2) Inactivation enzymes
(3) Destruction of spores
(4) Enhancement of colour
Answer: (1) Removal of undesirable odours
Explanation
Vacreation is a specialized steam-distillation process under vacuum
used predominantly to strip out volatile weed, feed, and other undesirable off-odors
from cream before churning.
Q14
Which of the following has highest ash content?
(1) Acid whey
(2) Paneer whey
(3) Rennet whey
(4) Demineralized whey
Answer: (1) Acid whey
Explanation
During acid coagulation (e.g., for cottage cheese or casein), colloidal
calcium phosphate is dissolved out of the casein micelle into the serum, giving acid
whey a significantly higher ash content than rennet whey.
Q15
The alternative addition to potassium nitrate in mold ripened cheese is
(1) Lactoperoxidase
(2) Lactase
(3) Lysozyme
(4) Aldolase
Answer: (3) Lysozyme
Explanation
Lysozyme (extracted from egg whites) specifically attacks the cell walls
of Clostridium species and is used safely to prevent 'late blowing' in cheese without
the regulatory issues of nitrates.
Q16
The fat globule membrane is composed of
(1) Phospholipids and lipoproteins
(2) Alpha-lactalbumin and Beta-lactoglobulin
(3) Casein and lactose
(4) Serum protein and whey proteins
Answer: (1) Phospholipids and lipoproteins
Explanation
The Milk Fat Globule Membrane (MFGM) is a complex, tri-layer
structure composed primarily of unique phospholipids, lipoproteins, and specialized
enzymes that stabilize the emulsion.
Q17
Which one of the following is not the theory of churning?
(1) Fisher and Hooker's phase reversal theory
(2) Rahn's foam theory
(3) King's autofloation theory
(4) Nichol's and Hodgson theory
Answer: (4) Nichol's and Hodgson theory
Explanation
Fisher & Hooker proposed phase reversal. Rahn proposed foam theory.
King proposed modern unified churning theory. There is no recognized 'Nichol's and
Hodgson' theory of churning.
Q18
The level and method of salting have a major influence on
(1) pH changes in cheese
(2) Proteolysis in cheese
(3) Lipolysis in cheese
(4) Free fatty acid content in cheese
Answer: (2) Proteolysis in cheese
Explanation
Salt controls the moisture, checks lactic acid bacteria, and specifically
regulates the activity of proteolytic enzymes during ripening, heavily influencing the
final texture and body.
Q19
Pindi, Dhap and Danedar are the variants of
(1) Burfi
(2) Paneer
(3) Chhana
(4) Khoa
Answer: (4) Khoa
Explanation
Khoa is classified into three types: Pindi (for peda/burfi), Dhap (for
gulab jamun/pantua), and Danedar (granular, for kalakand).
Q20
Which of the following statements in not true?
(1) Buffalo milk shows faster rennetting
(2) Buffalo milk shows faster syneresis
(3) Buffalo milk shows poor cheddaring
(4) Buffalo milk shows faster flavor development in cheese
Answer: (4) Buffalo milk shows faster flavor development in cheese
Explanation
Due to its higher fat content, larger casein micelles, and higher mineral
load, biochemical changes and flavor development during ripening in buffalo milk
cheese are noticeably SLOWER than in cow milk cheese.
Q21
Eyes is a defect in
(1) Gouda cheese
(2) Emmentaler cheese
(3) Roquefort cheese
(4) Edam cheese
Answer: (3) Roquefort cheese
Explanation
While large 'eyes' are desirable and required in Emmentaler, any gas
hole formation in an internally mold-ripened cheese like Roquefort (or Cheddar) is
considered a severe defect. (Note: NTA key often flags Gouda or Edam, but Roquefort
is absolutely non-eyed).
Q22
Late blowing in processed cheese is controlled by using
(1) Sodium citrate
(2) Sorbic acid
(3) Citric acid
(4) Nisin
Answer: (4) Nisin
Explanation
Nisin is a bacteriocin produced by Lactococcus lactis that is highly
effective at inhibiting the outgrowth of heat-resistant Clostridium spores, preventing
late blowing in processed cheese.
Q23
For better granulation, ghee should be slowly cooled to
(1) 50°C in 6-8 hours
(2) 40°C in 4-6 hours
(3) 28°C in 2-3 hours
(4) 15°C in 1-2 hours
Answer: (3) 28°C in 2-3 hours
Explanation
To develop large, distinct grains (crystals of high-melting triglycerides),
ghee must be cooled very slowly to just above room temperature (approx 28°C)
without agitation.
Q24
In which method of ghee manufacture highest energy is required?
(1) Indigenous (Desi)
(2) Direct cream
(3) Creamery
(4) Pre-stratification
Answer: (1) Indigenous (Desi)
Explanation
The indigenous method involves boiling raw, un-concentrated butter or
cream, requiring the evaporation of massive amounts of water, thus consuming the
absolute highest amount of thermal energy.
Q25
Which of the following grades has the highest purity of lactose?
(1) Technical grade lactose
(2) Food grade lactose
(3) Crude lactose
(4) Pharmaceutical lactose
Answer: (4) Pharmaceutical lactose
Explanation
Pharmaceutical grade lactose undergoes extensive recrystallization
and purification to achieve near 100% purity, far exceeding food or technical grades.
Q26
Ripening index in cheese is calculated as
(1) Total whey protein / Total protein x 100
(2) soluble protein / Total protein x 100
(3) Total casein / Total protein x 100
(4) Total SNF / Total protein x 100
Answer: (2) soluble protein / Total protein x 100
Explanation
The extent of ripening (proteolysis) is analytically quantified by
measuring the amount of nitrogen (protein) that has become soluble compared to the
total nitrogen in the cheese.
Q27
The first phase of freezing process of ice cream mix account for
freezing of water to about
(1) 15-20%
(2) 25-28%
(3) 33-67%
(4) 70-85%
Answer: (3) 33-67%
Explanation
Inside the continuous freezer barrel (the first phase), only about 33% to
67% (roughly half) of the water is actually frozen into ice. The rest is frozen later in
the hardening tunnel.
Q28
The first principle of HACCP is
(1) Conduct a hazard analysis
(2) Identify critical control point
(3) Establish critical limits
(4) Establish verification procedure
Answer: (1) Conduct a hazard analysis
Explanation
The 7 Principles of HACCP begin strictly with Principle 1: Conduct a
Hazard Analysis (identifying biological, chemical, and physical hazards).
Q29
Total Quality Management (TQM) is a concept which goes beyond
(1) ISO 14000
(2) ISO 22000
(3) ISO 9001
(4) ISO 8402
Answer: (3) ISO 9001
Explanation
ISO 9001 defines a Quality Management System. TQM is a broader,
holistic philosophy embedding a culture of continuous improvement across every
single level of an organization, going beyond basic ISO 9001 compliance.
Q30
Which of the following is NOT the part of the Quality Control
Management System (QCMS)?
(1) Quality planning process
(2) Quality control process
(3) Harmonization of national standards with codex standards
(4) Quality improvement process
Answer: (3) Harmonization of national standards with codex standards
Explanation
Harmonization of national standards with Codex Alimentarius is a
macro-level government/regulatory function, not an internal operational part of a
plant's QCMS.
Q31
Greasy body and texture defect in butter is due to
(1) Low wash water temperature
(2) Over working
(3) Low salt content
(4) Use of too much colour
Answer: (2) Over working
Explanation
Excessive mechanical working (kneading) of the butter physically
breaks down the crystal network, resulting in a soft, salvy, or greasy texture that
melts too easily.
Q32
In case of aluminum laminated cartons, the aluminum foil
(1) Acts as gas and light barrier
(2) Provides liquid barrier
(3) Provides mechanical strength
(4) Enables the sealing process
Answer: (1) Acts as gas and light barrier
Explanation
Aluminum foil is completely impermeable. Even at microscopic
thicknesses, it provides an absolute, 100% barrier against light and oxygen (gas)
transmission.
Q33
In active packaging system, carbon dioxide absorbers/emitters are used
in
(1) Coffee, fresh meat, fish, nuts, etc.
(2) Fruits and vegetables
(3) Ready meals and beverages
(4) Cereals
Answer: (1) Coffee, fresh meat, fish, nuts, etc.
Explanation
Fresh roasted coffee aggressively releases CO2, which can burst
packages, necessitating absorbers. Some fresh meats use emitters to inhibit aerobic
bacterial growth.
Q34
Which of the following plastic films is the thickest?
(1) LDPE with 2 mil
(2) LLDPE with 0.01 inch
(3) Polystyrene with 25 microns
(4) Nylon with 150 gauge
Answer: (2) LLDPE with 0.01 inch
Explanation
1 mil = 0.001 inch = 25.4 microns. 150 gauge = 1.5 mil. Therefore: LDPE
= 2 mil. PS = ~1 mil. Nylon = 1.5 mil. LLDPE = 0.01 inch = 10 mil. LLDPE is the thickest.
Q35
Name the membrane system which does function of simultaneous
concentration and demineralization.
(1) Microfiltration
(2) Ultrafiltration
(3) Reverse osmosis
(4) Nanofiltration
Answer: (4) Nanofiltration
Explanation
Nanofiltration pores are small enough to hold back lactose and proteins
(concentrating them) but loose enough to allow water and monovalent salt ions to
bleed through (demineralizing).
Q36
Microfiltration is designed to retain particles in the range of
(1) 0.005 microns to 0.008 microns
(2) 0.10 microns to 5 microns
(3) 0.009 microns to 0.05 microns
(4) 0.05 microns to 0.08 microns
Answer: (2) 0.10 microns to 5 microns
Explanation
Microfiltration uses highly porous membranes (0.1 to 5 µm) specifically
to intercept large suspended particles like bacteria, spores, and fat globules.
Q37
Name the membrane process which uses membranes with larger pores
to allow permeation of many organic compounds such as sugars
(1) Reverse osmosis
(2) Ultrafiltration
(3) Nanofiltration
(4) Microfiltration
Answer: (2) Ultrafiltration
Explanation
Ultrafiltration (UF) retains large macromolecules like proteins but
allows water, salts, and smaller organic compounds like lactose (sugars) to freely
permeate.
Q38
Which one of the following is NOT a flavor defect related to oxidation of
butter?
(1) Metallic
(2) Bitter
(3) Fishy
(4) Tallow
Answer: (2) Bitter
Explanation
Metallic, fishy, and tallowy flavors are all classic, sequential stages of
lipid auto-oxidation. Bitterness is typically a proteolytic (microbial/enzymatic) defect,
not oxidative.
Q39
The formation of gas holes or eyes throughout in Emmentaler cheese is
due to
(1) Lactobacillus acidophilus
(2) Propionibacterium shermanii
(3) Lactococcus lactis
(4) Bacillus subtilis
Answer: (2) Propionibacterium shermanii
Explanation
During secondary ripening in a warm room, Propionibacterium
freudenreichii subsp. shermanii ferments lactic acid into propionic acid, acetic acid,
and massive amounts of CO2 gas, forming the eyes.
Q40
The major flavouring compound present in Dahi is
(1) Acetone
(2) Acetaldehyde
(3) Diacetyl
(4) Methylketones
Answer: (3) Diacetyl
Explanation
Diacetyl (2,3-butanedione) is the principal volatile compound
responsible for the rich, buttery, and acidic flavor profile of Indian Dahi.
Q41
Which of the following chemicals is added to control blowing problem in
Gouda cheese?
(1) Potassium chloride
(2) Hydrogen peroxide
(3) Potassium nitrate
(4) Potassium metabisulphite
Answer: (3) Potassium nitrate
Explanation
Unlike Emmental, Gouda does not rely on propionic bacteria.
Therefore, Potassium or Sodium Nitrate is routinely added to inhibit Clostridium
spores and prevent late blowing.
Q42
Rancid off flavor in fat rich dairy products is developed due to
hydrolysis of
(1) Lactose
(2) Fat
(3) Casein
(4) Whey protein
Answer: (2) Fat
Explanation
Lipase enzymes hydrolyze the triglyceride ester bonds in milk fat,
releasing short-chain free fatty acids (like butyric acid) which possess an intensely
pungent, rancid odor.
Q43
The cooked flavor of milk on heating develops due to the release of SH
groups by heat denaturation of
(1) Whey proteins
(2) Alpha-casein
(3) Beta-casein
(4) Kappa-casein
Answer: (1) Whey proteins
Explanation
Temperatures above 75°C denature whey proteins (specifically beta-
lactoglobulin), exposing its buried sulfhydryl (-SH) groups, which release volatile
sulfides creating the 'cooked' or 'cabbage' flavor.
Q44
The low heating temperature during clarification of ghee adversely
affects which one of the following?
(1) Colour
(2) Vitamin A content
(3) Keeping quality
(4) Vitamin C content
Answer: (3) Keeping quality
Explanation
Heating ghee to a high temperature (110-120°C) is necessary to
completely drive off moisture and generate protective antioxidant compounds (from
Maillard browning). Low heat leaves moisture, ruining keeping quality.
Q45
Fat losses in buttermilk are more in which of the following methods?
(1) Desi method
(2) Creamery butter
(3) Direct cream method
(4) Continuous butter making method
Answer: (1) Desi method
Explanation
The indigenous (Desi) method involves churning unstandardized, often
warm, fermented whole milk or raw cream, leading to highly inefficient fat recovery
and massive fat loss in buttermilk.
Q46
Maximum possible overrun in butter is
(1) 15%
(2) 20%
(3) 25%
(4) 30%
Answer: (3) 25%
Explanation
Overrun in butter is calculated based on the difference between the
mass of butter produced and the mass of pure fat churned. Standard butter is 80%
fat. (100-80)/80 = 25% theoretical max overrun.
Q47
In butter making, increasing viscosity of cream results in
(1) Increase in curd content
(2) Hard butter
(3) Delayed churning
(4) Shortened churning
Answer: (3) Delayed churning
Explanation
If cream is excessively viscous, the mechanical agitation cannot
effectively smash the fat globules together. This prevents proper phase inversion,
severely delaying churning time.
Q48
Butter fat stored in Nitrogen would have good keeping quality when
oxygen content is not more than
(1) 0.0 ppm
(2) 0.5 ppm
(3) 1.0 ppm
(4) 1.5 ppm
Answer: (2) 0.5 ppm
Explanation
To effectively halt lipid auto-oxidation in anhydrous milk fat stored
under an inert gas blanket, the residual headspace oxygen must be strictly
maintained below 0.5 ppm.
Q49
Which of the following materials is commonly used as closure for PET
bottles?
(1) PP
(2) BOPP
(3) PET
(4) HDPE
Answer: (1) PP
Explanation
Polypropylene (PP) provides the necessary rigidity, durability, and
thread-holding strength required for screw caps on beverage bottles.
Q50
Critical head space oxygen content maintained during whole milk
powder packaging is
(1) 1.0%
(2) 2.0%
(3) 3.0%
(4) 4.0%
Answer: (2) 2.0%
Explanation
Whole milk powder is highly susceptible to fat oxidation. Gas flushing
(nitrogen/vacuum) is used to drive the headspace oxygen strictly below 2.0% to
ensure a long shelf life.
Q51
The four layers of retort pouch from inside to outside are
(1) LDPE/HDPE/PP/aluminum
(2) LDPE/Aluminum/PP/PET
(3) PP/Polyamide/aluminum/PET
(4) PP/Aluminum/Nylon/PET
Answer: (4) PP/Aluminum/Nylon/PET
Explanation
Standard retort pouch: Inside food-contact layer is PP (heat seal/temp
resistant). Then Aluminum foil (absolute barrier). Then Nylon (puncture resistance).
Outside is PET (printability/strength).
Q52
Which of the following is an example of ethylene absorber?
(1) Alumina
(2) Silica gel
(3) Potassium permanganate
(4) Ferrous sulphate
Answer: (3) Potassium permanganate
Explanation
Potassium permanganate (KMnO4) is a powerful oxidizing agent that
chemically reacts with and destroys ethylene gas, vastly extending the shelf life of
climacteric fruits.
Q53
Which of the following is used as refrigerant in cryogenic freezing?
(1) Nitrogen
(2) Carbon dioxide
(3) Liquid Nitrogen and liquid Carbon dioxide
(4) Oxygen
Answer: (3) Liquid Nitrogen and liquid Carbon dioxide
Explanation
Both Liquid Nitrogen (-196°C) and Liquid Carbon Dioxide (-78°C solid)
are universally used in food processing for ultra-rapid cryogenic freezing.
Q54
The nozzles of pressure nozzle atomizers are made of the following
material
(1) Tungsten carbide
(2) Chromium
(3) Nickel
(4) Steel
Answer: (1) Tungsten carbide
Explanation
To withstand the extreme, catastrophic abrasive forces of liquid
blasting through a microscopic hole at 200+ bar, the orifice insert MUST be made of
ultra-hard Tungsten Carbide.
Q55
The separation efficiency of cyclone separator is inversely related to its
(1) Diameter
(2) Square of diameter
(3) Square root of its diameter
(4) Length of cyclone separator
Answer: (1) Diameter
Explanation
Centrifugal force (F = mv²/r). As the radius/diameter increases, the
centrifugal force throwing particles to the wall drops. Thus, efficiency is inversely
proportional to diameter.
Q56
Match: A. Sod. aluminum silicate (II. Free-flow), B. EDTA (V.
Sequestering), C. Nisin (I. Bacteriocin), E. Iodophor (IV. Sanitizer)
(1) A-IV, B-III, C-V, D-II, E-I
(2) A-III, B-I, C-II, D-IV, E-V
(3) A-V, B-IV, C-III, D-II, E-I
(4) A-II, B-V, C-I, D-III, E-IV
Answer: (4) A-II, B-V, C-I, D-III, E-IV
Explanation
Silicates prevent caking. EDTA binds metal ions. Nisin is an
antimicrobial peptide. Niacin is Vitamin B3. Iodophor is an iodine-based dairy
sanitizer.
Q57
Match: A. Autoxidation (IV. Peroxides), B. Lipid hydrolysis (III. FFA), C.
Proteolysis (V. Soluble N), D. Maillard (I. HMF), E. Denaturation (II. Sulphydryl)
(1) A-V, B-II, C-IV, D-I, E-III
(2) A-IV, B-III, C-V, D-I, E-II
(3) A-II, B-V, C-IV, D-III, E-I
(4) A-I, B-IV, C-V, D-II, E-III
Answer: (2) A-IV, B-III, C-V, D-I, E-II
Explanation
Autoxidation generates peroxides. Hydrolysis yields Free Fatty Acids.
Proteolysis generates soluble nitrogen. Maillard forms HMF. Denaturation unfolds
proteins, exposing buried Sulphydryl groups.
Q58
Match: A. Dahi (IV. Diacetyl), B. Yoghurt (III. Acetaldehyde), C. Cooked (I.
Sulphydryls), D. Sunlight (V. Methanal - wait, actually Methional), E. Oxidized
(II. Aldehydes/Ketones)
(1) A-II, B-III, C-IV, D-I, E-V
(2) A-IV, B-II, C-I, D-III, E-V
(3) A-III, B-V, C-IV, D-II, E-I
(4) A-IV, B-III, C-I, D-V, E-II
Answer: (4) A-IV, B-III, C-I, D-V, E-II
Explanation
Dahi = Diacetyl. Yogurt = Acetaldehyde. Cooked = Sulphydryls (H2S).
Sunlight flavor = Methional (amino acid breakdown). Oxidized = Aldehydes/Ketones.
Q59
Match: Adulterant Tests. A. Sugar (II. Resorcinol), B. H2O2 (V. Ferric
chloride/Vanadium), C. Formaldehyde (V. Ferric chloride - actually Hehner
test), E. Urea (I. p-dimethyl amino benzaldehyde - DMAB)
(1) A-II, B-IV, C-V, D-III, E-I
(2) A-III, B-II, C-I, D-IV, E-V
(3) A-I, B-II, C-V, D-III, E-IV
(4) A-II, B-V, C-IV, D-III, E-I
Answer: (1) A-II, B-IV, C-V, D-III, E-I

Sugar = Resorcinol (Baudouin/Seliwanoff). Urea = DMAB reagent. H2O2
= p-phenyl diamine. Maltodextrin = Iodine. Formaldehyde = Ferric chloride (Hehner).
Q60
Match: A. Gustatory (II. Taste), B. Olfactory (III. Odour), C. Kinesthetic (V.
Deep pressure/Texture), D. Vestibular (I. Balance), E. Auditory (IV. Sound)
(1) A-II, B-III, C-IV, D-I, E-V
(2) A-V, B-IV, C-I, D-II, E-III
(3) A-II, B-III, C-V, D-I, E-IV
(4) A-IV, B-III, C-V, D-II, E-I
Answer: (3) A-II, B-III, C-V, D-I, E-IV
Explanation
Standard physiological sensory mapping. Gustatory = taste buds.
Olfactory = smell. Kinesthetic = mouthfeel/resistance. Vestibular = inner ear/balance.
Auditory = hearing/crunch.
Q61
If 'mg' is mass of dry steam and 'mf' is mass of water in suspension,
then dryness fraction of steam is equal to
(1) mg/(mg + mf)
(2) mf/(mg + mf)
(3) mg/mf
(4) mf/mg
Answer: (1) mg/(mg + mf)
Explanation
Dryness fraction (x) defines the quality of wet steam. It is the mass of
actual dry vapor divided by the total mass of the mixture (vapor + liquid droplets).
Q62
If the value of n = 0 in the general law pv^n = C, then the process is
called
(1) Isochoric process
(2) Isobaric process
(3) Isothermal process
(4) Isentropic process
Answer: (2) Isobaric process
Explanation
If n=0, the equation becomes pv^0 = C, which is p(1) = C. Since pressure
(p) equals a constant, it is an isobaric (constant pressure) process.
Q63
The heating of gas at constant pressure is governed by
(1) Charle's Law
(2) Joule's Law
(3) Boyle's Law
(4) Gay-Lussac Law
Answer: (1) Charle's Law
Explanation
Charles's Law states that at a constant pressure, the volume of an ideal
gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature (V/T = k).
