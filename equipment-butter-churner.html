Continuous Butter Making Machine

A continuous buttermaking system converts conditioned cream into butter in a controlled, closed process. Churning, butter–buttermilk separation, squeeze-drying, intensive working, vacuum treatment, salt dosing and final moisture adjustment occur as a continuous sequence rather than as separate batch operations.

01 • WHAT IS A CONTINUOUS BUTTERMAKER?

From conditioned cream to a continuous ribbon of butter

The machine is designed around the same fundamental transformation as conventional churning, but the individual operations are arranged in a continuous mechanical line. The cream is fed continuously, butter grains are formed rapidly, buttermilk is removed, and the butter is progressively worked until its structure, moisture distribution and final composition are suitable for storage or packaging.

Core process concept

Churning destabilises the fat emulsion and produces butter grains. The following working sections consolidate these grains into a continuous butter phase while removing excess buttermilk and distributing the aqueous phase finely throughout the butter.

Closed continuous processControlled working intensityFine moisture dispersion

Why continuous manufacture?

Compared with batch manufacture, a continuous system can provide tighter process control, more uniform body and texture, consistent salt/moisture distribution, reduced floor space and labour, and direct integration with downstream butter handling and packaging.

03 • NUMBERED CONSTRUCTION

Four primary sections of the buttermaker

The first machine view shows the main continuous manufacturing train from churning through the second working section.

Continuous buttermaking machine: cream enters the churning cylinder, followed by separation, squeeze-drying and the second working section.
1

Churning cylinder

A double-cooled cylinder fitted with high-speed beaters. Conditioned cream is rapidly churned here, destabilising the fat emulsion and producing butter grains.

2

Separation section

The butter grains and buttermilk enter this first working/separation zone. Buttermilk is removed while the butter begins to consolidate and move forward.

3

Squeeze-drying section

Butter passes through a conical channel and perforated restriction/plate arrangement. Mechanical squeezing removes remaining buttermilk and starts intensive working.

4

Second working section

The butter is further worked after separation. Its working intensity can be controlled so that the developing butter body and moisture dispersion reach the desired condition.

04 • VACUUM WORKING TRAIN

The final conditioning section

After the main working stages, the butter passes through injection, vacuum treatment and final working before leaving the machine. This section is critical for air removal, final moisture adjustment and product consistency.

Vacuum working section: injection, vacuum treatment, final working and moisture control.
5

Injection section

A dosing point where salt or another permitted ingredient can be introduced into the butter stream. Accurate dosing and good dispersion are essential for uniform finished-product composition.

6

Vacuum working section

Connected to a vacuum system. Vacuum treatment reduces the air content of the butter and supports a structure comparable in air content to conventionally churned butter.

7

Final working stage

A series of smaller working chambers separated by perforated plates. Different perforation sizes and impeller arrangements provide progressive treatment of the butter mass.

8

Moisture control unit

Instrumentation can monitor moisture, salt, density and temperature at the outlet. Feedback from these measurements can support automatic control of final product conditions.

05 • CHURNING MECHANISM

How cream becomes butter grains

The churning stage is the rapid phase-conversion step. The objective is to create butter grains of suitable size and firmness without carrying excessive buttermilk with them.

1. Conditioned cream enters

Cream prepared and ripened for conventional butter manufacture is continuously fed to the double-cooled churning cylinder.

2. High-speed agitation

Rapidly rotating beaters create intense agitation. Air is incorporated into the cream and the fat-globule interface is disturbed.

3. Fat destabilisation

Fat globules collide and lose part of their protective membrane material. Liquid fat and crystalline fat contribute to the developing butter-grain structure.

4. Butter grains form

The dispersed fat aggregates into visible grains. The residence time is very short, so beater speed and cream condition must be controlled carefully.

06 • SEPARATION & WORKING

Why the butter is worked after churning

Churning creates the butter grains, but the final butter is not obtained simply by collecting those grains. The working stages consolidate them and redistribute the remaining aqueous phase.

Buttermilk removal

The butter grains are separated from the continuous buttermilk phase. Efficient drainage reduces retained liquid and limits unnecessary fat losses in the buttermilk stream.

Squeeze-drying

Mechanical pressure through channels and perforated plates removes remaining buttermilk and brings the grains into closer contact.

Moisture dispersion

Continued working distributes water finely through the continuous fat phase. The objective is a stable butter body without visible free-water droplets.

07 • WORKING INTENSITY

Why different sections run at different conditions

Working is not a single operation. The butter changes from loose grains into a cohesive mass, so the mechanical treatment is staged to avoid either insufficient consolidation or excessive working.

Early working

The first screw/working section performs separation and begins consolidation. Its action must be strong enough to remove liquid and join the butter grains, while still allowing efficient drainage.

Later working

Later sections refine the structure, distribute moisture and salt, and prepare the butter for final discharge. Different screw speeds, perforations and impeller forms can be used for controlled treatment.

08 • VACUUM TREATMENT

Why is a vacuum section required?

Air can remain dispersed in butter after churning and working. The vacuum working section is connected to a vacuum pump and reduces the air content of the butter.

Process significance

Vacuum treatment is not the same as moisture control. Vacuum primarily addresses the gas content, while the final working and control system manages the physical distribution and target level of moisture, salt and other monitored properties.

09 • FINAL WORKING & MOISTURE CONTROL

Four small final-working sections

The final working stage is divided into smaller sections separated by perforated plates. The perforation size and impeller form can be selected to give the butter the required treatment.

Progressive treatment

Each small chamber contributes additional working rather than applying the entire mechanical treatment in one location.

Final moisture adjustment

An injector can be located in the first small section for final moisture adjustment. With stable cream characteristics, the controlled process can maintain a very small deviation around the set moisture condition.

Online measurement

Moisture, salt content, density and temperature transmitters can be installed at the outlet, providing process information for automatic control.

12 • ADVANTAGES

Why continuous buttermaking is used

Process uniformity

Continuous flow and controlled working sections can produce consistent body, texture and moisture distribution.

Hygienic closed operation

The process can be arranged as a closed system, reducing unnecessary exposure between processing and packaging.

Compact production

Several operations are integrated into one continuous machine rather than requiring separate batch handling steps.

Automation

Online transmitters and control systems can monitor key product properties and support automatic adjustment.

Lower manual handling

Continuous movement reduces repeated manual transfer of butter grains between processing stages.

Direct packaging integration

The continuous butter ribbon can be transferred onward to silo and packaging systems.

14 • ENGINEERING SUMMARY

Remember the machine as four functional zones

Wet side

Churning → separation → squeeze-drying. This side forms the butter grains, removes buttermilk and begins consolidation.

Dry / finishing side

Working → injection → vacuum → final working → moisture control. This side develops the final butter structure and adjusts composition.
