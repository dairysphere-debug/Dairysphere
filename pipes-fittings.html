1. Introduction

A dairy piping system is a network of pipes, fittings, valves, instruments and supports used to

transport milk, milk products and other process/service media between different equipment in a dairy plant.

The product pipe system includes

  • Straight pipes
  • Bends
  • Tees
  • Reducers
  • Unions
  • Special fittings such as sight glasses and instrument fittings
  • Valves for stopping/directing flow
  • Valves for pressure/flow control
  • Pipe supports

For hygienic reasons, product-wetted parts are made primarily from stainless steel. AISI 304 and

AISI 316 are important grades used in dairy equipment.

2. Pipe Vs Sanitary Tube

Explain the distinction clearly

PIPE

  • Conventional pipe is generally designated by nominal bore/nominal pipe size.
  • Outside diameter and internal diameter depend on the standard and wall thickness.

SANITARY TUBE

  • Sanitary tubing is commonly designated by outside diameter (OD).
  • Its available internal diameter is affected by wall thickness.

REFERENCE EXAMPLE

A 2-inch stainless-steel pipe may have an outside diameter of 2.375 inches and, for Schedule 10S,

a resultant bore of 2.157 inches.

A 2-inch sanitary tube has an outside diameter of 2.000 inches and a 0.065-inch wall thickness,

giving a resultant bore of 1.870 inches.

4. Sanitary Requirements

A sanitary dairy pipeline should be

1. Seamless and preferably cold drawn.

2. Softened, descaled and pickled.

3. Polished internally and externally.

4. Clean, smooth and free from surface defects.

5. Straight and free from longitudinal grooves.

6. Made with fitting material compatible with the pipe.

7. Designed so condensate does not create contamination risk.

8. Sized to allow required flow without excessive pressure loss.

9. Drainable.

10. Designed with minimal pockets/dead spaces.

11. Easy to clean and maintain.

DAIRY HYGIENIC DESIGN — DO / DON'T VISUAL

DO

  • Smooth surfaces
  • Smooth welds
  • Proper slope
  • Drainable layout
  • Accessible fittings
  • Hygienic connections

DON'T

  • Sharp internal corners
  • Dead pockets
  • Rough welds
  • Unnecessary fittings
  • Poor drainage
  • Product stagnation

5. Pipe Sizing

Pipe selection depends on

  • Flow rate
  • Product characteristics
  • Flow velocity
  • Pressure drop
  • Installation cost
  • Cleaning/drainage requirements

"Approximate capacities reproduced from the supplied Dairy Engineering course reference."

Important concept

  • Oversizing increases installation cost.
  • Undersizing increases pressure drop.
  • Selection criterion includes flow velocity and cost.
  • Next larger size may be selected for heavy liquids such as cream/ice-cream mix and for gravity flow,

according to the course reference.

6. Pipe Layout & Slope

Good piping layout

  • Keep piping lengths to a minimum.
  • Do not create operator hazards.
  • Keep valves/instruments accessible.
  • Provide adequate individual/group support.
  • Design for drainage.
  • Minimize pockets.
  • Provide adequate drain valves.

SLOPE

The supplied Dairy Engineering reference states that a slope of 0.3–0.5% is sufficient for

post-cleaning drainage and gravity flow of low-viscosity fluids.

10. Gaskets

Definition

A gasket is a sealing element used between mating surfaces to prevent leakage.

Dairy gasket requirements

  • Flexible
  • Fat resistant
  • Detergent resistant
  • Sanitizer resistant
  • Suitable for required temperature
  • Non-toxic/product-contact suitable where applicable

11. Glass Piping

Uses

  • Product visibility
  • Sight glasses
  • Level gauges
  • Light glasses
  • Selected special applications

Properties/limitations

  • Visibility is the major advantage.
  • Glass is brittle and fragile.
  • Suitable protection may be required.
  • The supplied course reference specifies non-splinter glass for relevant dairy applications.

15. Pipe Supports

Purpose

  • Prevent sagging
  • Maintain alignment
  • Reduce vibration
  • Support valves/fittings
  • Accommodate thermal movement

The course reference states that tubing should be supported regularly, near valves and at changes

in direction. Stainless-steel support materials are preferred, and floating supports can help

accommodate temperature changes.

18. Installation

Before installation

  • Prepare plant layout.
  • Identify equipment.
  • Prepare P&ID.
  • Assign pipe/line numbers.
  • Identify valves and instruments.
  • Consider elevations affecting operation.
  • Consider pump NPSH where relevant.
  • Prepare detailed piping/isometric drawing.

20. Quick Revision

Use flash-card style questions

Q: Which stainless-steel grades are important in dairy piping?

A: AISI 304 and AISI 316/316L.

Q: Why are sanitary pipes polished?

A: To provide smooth surfaces and facilitate hygienic cleaning.

Q: What is the recommended slope given in the course reference for post-cleaning drainage and

gravity flow of low-viscosity fluids?

A: 0.3–0.5%.

Q: What is the function of a reducer?

A: To connect pipes/tubes of different diameters.

Q: What is the function of a sight glass?

A: To allow visual checking of the product.

Q: What is a gasket?

A: A sealing element used to prevent leakage between mating surfaces.

Q: What is a mixproof valve used for?

A: To keep different media, such as product and CIP solution, separated while allowing controlled

routing through connected piping.

Q: What is the difference between isolation and control valves?

A: Isolation valves stop/permit flow; control valves regulate flow or pressure.

Q: Why are pipe supports required?

A: To support the pipeline, maintain alignment, reduce vibration and accommodate movement.
