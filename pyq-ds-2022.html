2022 Dairy Science — Solved PYQ

This document contains the full 120-question set for the 2022 Dairy Science paper. The content, answers and explanations are reproduced from the supplied source PDF; no unsupported corrections have been inserted.

Source note: The answers/explanations shown here follow the supplied PDF. Where the source says Data Unavailable or a question is dropped, that status is retained.
Q1
…......... lacks Leuconostoc as the flavour producer.
B and L culture
D and O culture
BD and DL culture
B and O culture
Answer: 2. D and O culture
Explanation
The 'O' culture (homofermentative lactococci only) and 'D' culture (contains diacetylactis but NO leuconostoc) both lack Leuconostoc as a flavor producer .
Q2
Which of the following amino acids can act as a source for acetaldehyde production by lactococci ?
Valine
Leucine
Lysine
Threonine
Answer: 4. Threonine
Explanation
Threonine acts as the primary substrate. The enzyme Threonine aldolase cleaves threonine directly into acetaldehyde and glycine.
Q3
Which of the following is not a concentrated fermented milk product?
Viili
Ymer
Skyr
Shrikand
Answer: 1. Viili
Explanation
Skyr , Ymer , and Shrikhand are all concentrated by draining whey (straining/centrifugation). Viili is a traditional Nordic fermented milk that is NOT concentrated.
Q4
According to FSSAI flavoured fermented milks are composite milk products obtained from fermented milks and shall contain a maximum ........ percentage (m/m) of permitted non dairy ingredients.
30
40
50
60
Answer: 3. 50
Explanation
According to FSSAI (Food Safety and Standards Regulations), flavored fermented milks can contain a maximum of 50% permitted non-dairy ingredients (like fruit pulp, sugar , etc.).
Q5
Lactic acid bacteria that can grow in the presence of 18% sodium chloride are
Micrococcus
Pediococcus
Lactococcus
Tetragenococcus
Answer: 4. Tetragenococcus
Explanation
Tetragenococcus species (formerly Pediococcus halophilus) are extreme halophiles, uniquely capable of growing in environments with up to 18% NaCl, commonly found in soy sauce fermentations.
Q6
Which among the following is not a mobile genetic element?
Insertion sequences
Plasmids
Transposons
Introns
Answer: 2. Plasmids
Explanation
Plasmids are extrachromosomal DNA, not 'mobile elements' in the genomic sense (like transposons or insertion sequences). Introns are non-coding sequences spliced out of mRNA, not mobile genetic elements.
Q7

Leuconostoc lactis and Lacticaseibacillus casei

Explanation
Lactococcus lactis and Lacticaseibacillus casei possess the complex genetic machinery to metabolize galactose via both the Leloir pathway and the Tagatose-6-phosphate pathway.

Q13
The interaction between propionibacteria and lactobacilli in Swiss cheese changes from ...................to ..........................when lactic acid is accumulated and lactobacilli starts to benefit from its removal by propionibacteria.
Commensalism to Mutualism
Mutualism to Commensalism
Amensalism to Competition
Competetion to Amensalism
Answer: 1. Commensalism to Mutualism
Explanation
Initially, the relationship is Commensalism (Propionibacteria benefit from Lactobacilli producing lactate). When lactate builds up to toxic levels, its removal by Propionibacteria benefits Lactobacilli, shifting to Mutualism.
Q14
In the scheme for microbiological testing for water,temperatures for taking colony count are ................ and .............
42 C and 30 C
37 C and 30 C
30 C and 22 C
37 C and 22 C
Answer: 4. 37 C and 22 C
Explanation
Standard microbiological water quality testing mandates performing the Heterotrophic Plate Count (HPC) at two distinct temperatures: 37°C (to detect mammalian pathogens) and 22°C (to detect environmental psychrotrophs).
Q15
The time from initial adsorption of phages to release of phages is called .............
Incubation period
Latent period
Withdrawal period
Burst period
Answer: 2. Latent period
Explanation
In virology, the 'Latent Period' is the exact time elapsed from the initial viral attachment/injection of DNA to the moment the bacterial cell violently bursts, releasing new phage particles.
Q16

Leuconostoc
Streptococcus
Answer: 1. Bifidobacterium

Bifidobacterium is a unique, high-GC Gram-positive genus belonging to the phylum Actinobacteria, with its genomic DNA base composition heavily skewed to 57-65% G+C.
Q17
Which statement is not true for Pseudolysogeny?
Pseudolusogeny is also known as a phage carrier state
Pseudolysogeny occurs when a bacterial culture carries a temperate phage while maintaining an active population.
Pseudolysogeny occurs when a bacterial culture carries a lytic phage while maintaining an active population.
Repeated culture purification helps in eliminating phages from a pseudolysogenenous culture.
Answer: 2. Pseudolysogeny occurs when a bacterial culture carries a temperate phage while maintaining an active population.
Explanation
Pseudolysogeny involves a temperate or lytic phage temporarily stalling its life cycle in a starved cell. It does NOT mean the culture carries a temperate phage in a stable, active manner (that is true lysogeny).
Q18
In the manufacture of Yakult, fermentation time given is ‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐‐.
24 hours
48 hours
72 hours
More than 72 hours
Answer: 4. More than 72 hours
Explanation
Yakult uses a highly specialized strain, *Lacticaseibacillus paracasei Shirota*, which is incredibly slow-growing. To reach the massive required probiotic cell counts, fermentation is extended for more than 72 hours.
Q19
Unable to grow at 45 C,but able to grow in 0.3% Methylene blue and capable of hydrolysing arginine is true for ............................. .
Leuconostoc
Lactococcus
Bifidobacterium
Brevibacterium
Answer: 2. Lactococcus
Explanation
Lactococcus species (specifically L. lactis) cannot grow at 45°C, but they are highly tolerant of 0.3% Methylene blue and possess the ADI pathway to hydrolyze arginine into ammonia.
Q20
Which is not applicable for iodophores in dairy plant sanitation?
Shows good activity aganist yeast and mold.
More effective in alkaline pH
Less effective at elevated temperature.
Stable than chlorine in the presence of organic matter .
Answer: 2. More effective in alkaline pH
Explanation
Iodophors are acidic sanitizers. They are incredibly effective at low pH but rapidly lose all their germicidal power in alkaline (high pH) environments. Thus, stating they are 'more effective in alkaline pH' is completely false.
Q21
One of the causative organism responsible for toxi‐infection through milk products is
Bacillus subtilis
Pseudomonas aeruginosa
Clostridium perfringens
Klebsiella pneumoniae
Answer: 3. Clostridium perfringens
Explanation
*Clostridium perfringens* causes a 'toxi-infection'. The bacteria are ingested alive in food, survive the stomach, and then massive numbers sporulate in the gut, releasing a potent enterotoxin that causes illness.
Q22

Escherichia coli
Salmonella spp.
Enterococci spp.
Staphylococci spp.
Answer: 4. Staphylococci spp.
Explanation
The Thermonuclease (TNase) test is highly specific for detecting *Staphylococcus aureus* in foods. This heat-stable enzyme survives pasteurization even if the bacterial cells are killed, proving past contamination.

Q25
The defects encountered in UHT milk during storage is
loss of vitamins
mold button formation
gelation/coagulation
leakiness
Answer: 3. gelation/coagulation
Explanation
Age gelation (sweet coagulation) is a catastrophic defect in UHT milk. During prolonged room-temperature storage, the Maillard-damaged casein micelle complexes slowly detach and re-aggregate into a solid gel structure.
Q26
Which of the following known as “Cold sterilization” is used to reduce the microbial load of dairy liquids?
Tyndallization
Thermization
Microfiltration
Stassanization
Answer: 3. Microfiltration
Explanation
Microfiltration utilizes incredibly fine ceramic membranes (1.4 µm) to physically sieve out 99.9% of bacteria and spores from skim milk without applying any thermal damage, earning it the title 'Cold Sterilization'.
Q27
Given below are two statements, one is labelled as Assertion A and the other is labelled as Reason R Assertion A : Standardization practice in dairy industry is unsafe from microbiological point of view Reason R : The separation is carried out at around 40 C which is a congenial temperature for microbial growth and the cream and skim milk obtained, if used for standardization, would seed high number of microorganisms In light of the above statements, choose the correct answer from the options given below

Explanation
Both Assertion and Reason are true. Centrifugal separation is typically performed at 40°C-50°C. This perfectly warm temperature acts as a massive incubator , allowing rapid microbial multiplication in the skim and cream streams used for standardizing.
Q28
Ingestion of pre formed toxins already synthesized in the food brings about poisoning syndrome in consumer called;
Food Infection
Food Intoxication
Toxi‐Infection
Immune deficiency
Answer: 2. Food Intoxication
Explanation
A 'Food Intoxication' occurs strictly when a pathogen (like *S. aureus* or *C. botulinum*) grows in the food and secretes a toxin. The consumer eats the poison, not necessarily the live bacteria, causing rapid illness.

Q35

Q39

Q41

Q43
The sweet curdling in pasteurized milk may be due to

Explanation
*Bacillus subtilis* spores easily survive pasteurization. Once milk cools, they germinate and secrete a powerful rennet-like protease that swiftly coagulates the casein without producing any acid (hence 'sweet' curdling).

Q117

Acetic acid

Answer: 1. Citric acid

In fresh, unfermented raw milk, Citric Acid (present as citrate salts) is by far the most abundant organic acid, naturally present at levels of approximately 0.15% to 0.20%.
Q118
Lactose in milk occurs in form of
True solution
Colloidal dispersion
Associated with milk proteins
Suspension form
Answer: 1. True solution
Explanation
Milk is a complex physical system. Fat exists as an emulsion, proteins exist as a colloidal dispersion, but Lactose and mineral salts are dissolved entirely at the molecular level, forming a perfect True Solution.
