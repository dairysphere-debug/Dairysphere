DAIRY EQUIPMENT ATLAS • POWDER TECHNOLOGY

Spray Dryer

A large-scale drying system that converts concentrated liquid dairy feeds into powders by atomising the feed into fine droplets and rapidly evaporating water with hot air.

AtomisationDrying chamberPowder recoveryFluid bedFines return

What the spray dryer does

Spray drying turns a liquid or slurry into a dry powder by creating very small droplets and exposing them to hot drying air. Because droplets have a very large surface area relative to their volume, water can evaporate rapidly.

Why concentrate first?

Removing part of the water by membrane filtration and/or evaporation before spray drying reduces the load on the dryer and lowers energy demand.

02 • MAIN COMPONENTS

Spray dryer anatomy

1

Inlet air system

Filters, fans and air-conditioning equipment prepare the drying air before it enters the heater and distributor.

2

Air heater

Raises the drying-air temperature to the level required for rapid moisture removal.

3

Air distributor

Introduces hot air into the chamber with a controlled flow pattern around the atomised droplets.

4

Drying chamber

The main vessel where atomised droplets contact hot air and water evaporates. Dairy chambers commonly use a cylindrical body with a cone section.

5

Atomisation system

Converts the concentrate into fine droplets. High-pressure nozzles or a rotary atomiser may be used depending on dryer design.

6

Powder recovery

Cyclones, bag filters or combinations of separation equipment recover fine particles from the exhaust air.

7

Fluid bed

Provides additional drying and/or cooling and can support powder agglomeration and controlled final moisture properties.

8

Fines transport system

Returns recovered fines to the appropriate process zone when agglomeration or particle-size control requires it.

9

Powder sifter

Classifies the final powder and removes undesirable oversize particles before storage or packaging.

10

CIP & protection

Hygienic cleaning systems and fire/explosion protection are essential parts of a modern powder plant.

03 • ATOMISATION

The most important first transformation

Why make tiny droplets?

Atomisation creates a huge total surface area for heat and mass transfer. A concentrate is transformed into a cloud of droplets, allowing water at the droplet surface to evaporate rapidly when it contacts the hot air.

High-pressure nozzle vs rotary atomiser

Depending on product, capacity and powder requirements, spray dryers may use high-pressure nozzles or rotary atomisation. The selected atomiser controls droplet size and therefore strongly influences particle size and drying behaviour.

04 • DRYING CHAMBER

Heat and mass transfer inside the chamber

1

Droplet cooling

When the wet droplet enters the hot-air environment, rapid evaporation consumes heat and the droplet temperature remains far below the inlet-air temperature during the main evaporation period.

2

Moisture migration

As surface water evaporates, moisture from inside the droplet migrates toward the surface and continues to evaporate.

3

Particle formation

The droplet shrinks as water leaves. Its final structure, density, porosity and solubility depend on feed composition and drying conditions.

05 • SINGLE-STAGE VS MULTI-STAGE

How the dryer architecture changes

Single-stage drying

Most or all of the required moisture removal is achieved in the drying chamber. The arrangement is comparatively simple and can be suitable for straightforward products.

Multi-stage drying

Drying is distributed between the chamber and downstream equipment such as fluid beds. This provides greater control over final moisture, particle structure and powder properties and can improve energy efficiency.

07 • POWDER RECOVERY & FINES

Where the powder goes after drying

Cyclones and bag filters

Not all particles leave the chamber at the same size. Fine particles carried with exhaust air can be recovered by cyclones, bag filters or combinations of both.

Fines return

Recovered fines can be returned to the atomisation zone to interact with wet spray and form larger agglomerates when the product specification calls for improved wettability and dispersibility.

08 • DAIRY APPLICATIONS

Products commonly associated with spray drying

Skim milk powder

Liquid milk is concentrated and spray dried to produce a stable powder ingredient.

Whole milk powder

Whole milk concentrate is dried while managing fat-related powder properties and final moisture.

Whey powder

Whey concentrate can be spray dried after appropriate concentration and, where needed, crystallisation steps.

Milk proteins

Protein concentrates and isolates can be dried into convenient powdered ingredients.

Permeate

Milk or whey permeate streams can be converted into powder after concentration and suitable crystallisation/processing.

Instant/agglomerated powders

Downstream fluid-bed treatment and fines return can be used to improve powder functionality such as wettability and dispersibility.

Operator's mental model

Concentrate → filter/preheat → atomise → contact with hot air → evaporate water → separate powder → finish in fluid bed → sieve → store.
