Q1
The principal compound that is responsible for the stretch quality of
Mozzarella cheese is
(1) Dicalcium para-caseinate
(2) Monocalcium para-caseinate
(3) para-casein
(4) Calcium phosphate
Answer: (2) Monocalcium para-caseinate
Explanation
At pH 5.2-5.4, part of the colloidal calcium phosphate is dissolved,
forming monocalcium para-caseinate which provides the characteristic stretchability
(pasta filata) to Mozzarella. [Ref: Cheese Technology by Kosikowski]
Q2
In the manufacture of Cheddar cheese curd is milled at an acidity of-
(1) 0.45 to 0.50% LA
(2) 0.25 to 0.35% LA
(3) 0.55 to 0.65% LA
(4) 0.20 to 0.25% LA
Answer: (3) 0.55 to 0.65% LA
Explanation
In standard Cheddar cheese making, milling is done when the curd
reaches a titratable acidity of 0.55% to 0.65% LA to ensure proper texture and
moisture expulsion before salting. [Ref: Cheese Technology by Kosikowski]

Q3
Statement I: During manufacture of Mozzarella cheese, curd is stretched
at pH 5.2 to 5.4. Statement II: During manufacture of Pasta filata-type cheese,
curd is stretched at pH 5.8 to 6.2.
(1) Both true
(2) Both false
(3) I is true but II is false
(4) I is false but II is true
Answer: (3) I is true but II is false
Explanation
Mozzarella is stretched exactly between pH 5.2 and 5.4. Statement II is
false because ALL pasta filata cheeses (including Provolone, Caciocavallo) must be
stretched in this specific 5.2-5.4 range to form the monocalcium para-caseinate
matrix. [Ref: Cheese Technology]
Q4
Presence of excess fat in casein during drying may result in -
(1) high adhesive strength
(2) low adhesive strength
(3) brittleness
(4) browning
Answer: (2) low adhesive strength
Explanation
Casein is primarily used industrially as a high-strength glue/adhesive.
Excess milk fat acts as a hydrophobic lubricant, drastically reducing the cohesive and
adhesive strength of the dried casein powder. [Ref: Advanced Dairy Chemistry by P.F.
Fox]

Q6
Which one of the following additives is added to cheese milk to improve
the firmness of the curd?
(1) Potassium chloride
(2) Calcium chloride
(3) Sodium chloride
(4) Sodium citrate
Answer: (2) Calcium chloride
Explanation
Pasteurization precipitates soluble calcium. Adding 0.02% Calcium
Chloride (CaCl2) restores the soluble calcium balance, heavily improving rennet
action and resulting curd firmness. [Ref: Cheese Technology]

Q7
Which of the following is correct with regard to development of
bitterness in cheese? A. Poor quality milk, B. Low salt-to-moisture ratio, C.
High solids content, D. Poor quality starter
(1) A, B and C only
(2) B, C and D only
(3) A, B and D only
(4) A, C and D only
Answer: (3) A, B and D only
Explanation
Bitterness is caused by the accumulation of hydrophobic peptides. This
is promoted by poor quality milk (psychrotrophs), low salt (which fails to inhibit
bitter-producing enzymes), and highly proteolytic (poor) starter cultures. High solids
content (low moisture) actually *slows* enzymatic activity. [Ref: Cheese Technology]

to allow mold veins; gas 'eyes' are a severe defect. [Ref: Cheese Technology]

Q9
Which of the following is correct with regard to rennet clotting of milk? A.
Addition of Meito coagulating enzyme. B. Adding CaCl2 to decrease time. C.
Addition of NaCl for quick clotting. D. Use of sterilized milk.
(1) A, B and C only
(2) B, C and D only
(3) A and B only
(4) B and D only
Answer: (3) A and B only
Explanation
Meito (Rhizomucor miehei) is a known microbial rennet (A). CaCl2
decreases clotting time (B). NaCl actually *retards* rennet clotting (C is false).
Sterilized milk (UHT) binds whey proteins to casein, ruining rennet clotting (D is
false). [Ref: Dairy Chemistry]

Q11
Addition of 1.0 percent sodium chloride to milk has the following effect
on rennet clotting time (RCT).
(1) Decrease RCT
(2) Increase RCT
(3) No effect on RCT
(4) No clotting
Answer: (2) Increase RCT
Explanation
Adding Sodium Chloride (NaCl) directly to milk increases the ionic
strength, which interferes with the enzymatic action of rennet and the subsequent
aggregation of micelles, substantially increasing (delaying) the Rennet Clotting Time.
[Ref: Dairy Chemistry]

Q20
Which of the following is/are reason(s) of heat coagulation of milk
during khoa production? A. Altered salt balance. B. Decrease in pH. C.
Increase in total solids. D. High temperature. E. Continuous stirring.
(1) B and E only
(2) B, C and D only
(3) A, B, C and D only
(4) B only
Answer: (3) A, B, C and D only
Explanation
Khoa formation is caused by the combined forces of massive heat (D),
concentration of solids (C), the resulting drop in pH (B), and the destabilization of the
mineral salt balance (A). Stirring prevents burning but does not *cause* coagulation.
[Ref: Outlines of Dairy Technology]

Q31
Which of the following is NOT responsible for flat flavour in Cheddar
cheese?
(1) Low curing temperature
(2) Short ripening time
(3) Use of low fat milk
(4) Low cooking temperature
Answer: (4) Low cooking temperature
Explanation
Flat flavor means a lack of developed cheese taste. This is caused by
low fat (flavor solvent), short ripening (no breakdown), or low curing temp (inactive
enzymes). Low cooking temperature actually leaves excess moisture, promoting sour/
bitter, not flat flavors. [Ref: Cheese Technology]

Q36

Q40
The 'foam theory of churning' has been put forth by following
scientist(s):
(1) Rahn
(2) King
(3) Fisher and Hooker
(4) Van Dam and Holwerda
Answer: (1) Rahn
Explanation
Otto Rahn (1928) proposed the Foam Theory, stating that during
churning, milk fat globules gather at the air-water interface of air bubbles, where the
membrane is sheared off, causing the fat to clump when the bubble collapses. [Ref:
Outlines of Dairy Technology]
