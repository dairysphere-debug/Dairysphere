2022 Dairy Technology PYQ

120 questions • Answers and explanations in webpage text format.

Q1
The principal compound that is responsible for the stretch quality of Mozzarella cheese is
(1) Dicalcium para-caseinate
(2) Monocalcium para-caseinate
(3) para-casein
(4) Calcium phosphate
Answer: (2) Monocalcium para-caseinate
Explanation
At pH 5.2-5.4, part of the colloidal calcium phosphate is dissolved, forming monocalcium para-caseinate which provides the characteristic stretchability (pasta filata) to Mozzarella.
Q2
In the manufacture of Cheddar cheese curd is milled at an acidity of-
(1) 0.45 to 0.50% LA
(2) 0.25 to 0.35% LA
(3) 0.55 to 0.65% LA
(4) 0.20 to 0.25% LA
Answer: (3) 0.55 to 0.65% LA
Explanation
In standard Cheddar cheese making, milling is done when the curd reaches a titratable acidity of 0.55% to 0.65% LA to ensure proper texture and moisture expulsion before salting.
Q3
Statement I: During manufacture of Mozzarella cheese, curd is stretched at pH 5.2 to 5.4. Statement II: During manufacture of Pasta filata-type cheese, curd is stretched at pH 5.8 to 6.2.
(1) Both true
(2) Both false
(3) I is true but II is false
(4) I is false but II is true
Answer: (3) I is true but II is false
Explanation
Mozzarella is stretched exactly between pH 5.2 and 5.4. Statement II is false because ALL pasta filata cheeses (including Provolone, Caciocavallo) must be stretched in this specific 5.2-5.4 range to form the monocalcium para-caseinate matrix.
Q4
Presence of excess fat in casein during drying may result in -
(1) high adhesive strength
(2) low adhesive strength
(3) brittleness
(4) browning
Answer: (2) low adhesive strength
Explanation
Casein is primarily used industrially as a high-strength glue/adhesive. Excess milk fat acts as a hydrophobic lubricant, drastically reducing the cohesive and adhesive strength of the dried casein powder.
Q5
The annatto colour used in cheese making is
(1) Carotene
(2) Xanthophyll
(3) Bixin
(4) Norbixin
Answer: (4) Norbixin
Explanation
While Bixin is the primary pigment in annatto seeds, Norbixin is the water-soluble derivative of bixin that is specifically utilized to uniformly color the aqueous phase of cheese milk.
Q6
Which one of the following additives is added to cheese milk to improve the firmness of the curd?
(1) Potassium chloride
(2) Calcium chloride
(3) Sodium chloride
(4) Sodium citrate
Answer: (2) Calcium chloride
Explanation
Pasteurization precipitates soluble calcium. Adding 0.02% Calcium Chloride (CaCl2) restores the soluble calcium balance, heavily improving rennet action and resulting curd firmness.
Q7
Which of the following is correct with regard to development of bitterness in cheese? A. Poor quality milk, B. Low salt-to-moisture ratio, C. High solids content, D. Poor quality starter
(1) A, B and C only
(2) B, C and D only
(3) A, B and D only
(4) A, C and D only
Answer: (3) A, B and D only
Explanation
Bitterness is caused by the accumulation of hydrophobic peptides. This is promoted by poor quality milk (psychrotrophs), low salt (which fails to inhibit bitter-producing enzymes), and highly proteolytic (poor) starter cultures. High solids content (low moisture) actually *slows* enzymatic activity.
Q8
Eyes is a defect in-
(1) Gouda cheese
(2) Roquefort cheese
(3) Edam cheese
(4) Emmentalar cheese
Answer: (2) Roquefort cheese
Explanation
Emmentaler requires eyes. Gouda and Edam can have small 'mechanical' holes. However, Roquefort (a blue mold cheese) must be densely packed to allow mold veins; gas 'eyes' are a severe defect.
Q9
Which of the following is correct with regard to rennet clotting of milk? A. Addition of Meito coagulating enzyme. B. Adding CaCl2 to decrease time. C. Addition of NaCl for quick clotting. D. Use of sterilized milk.
(1) A, B and C only
(2) B, C and D only
(3) A and B only
(4) B and D only
Answer: (3) A and B only
Explanation
Meito (Rhizomucor miehei) is a known microbial rennet (A). CaCl2 decreases clotting time (B). NaCl actually *retards* rennet clotting (C is false). Sterilized milk (UHT) binds whey proteins to casein, ruining rennet clotting (D is false).
Q10
Which of the following statements is NOT true?
(1) Edam cheese is a dutch variety
(2) Buffalo milk clots faster than cow milk
(3) Quarg is a ripened cheese
(4) Chymosin is a coagulating enzyme
Answer: (3) Quarg is a ripened cheese
Explanation
Quarg (or Quark) is a fresh, unripened, acid-coagulated fresh cheese. It undergoes absolutely no curing or ripening process.
Q11
Addition of 1.0 percent sodium chloride to milk has the following effect on rennet clotting time (RCT).
(1) Decrease RCT
(2) Increase RCT
(3) No effect on RCT
(4) No clotting
Answer: (2) Increase RCT
Explanation
Adding Sodium Chloride (NaCl) directly to milk increases the ionic strength, which interferes with the enzymatic action of rennet and the subsequent aggregation of micelles, substantially increasing (delaying) the Rennet Clotting Time.
Q12
Which of the following is NOT a whey protein?
(1) Phosphatidile choline
(2) Immunoglobulin
(3) Bovine serum
(4) a-lactalbumin
Answer: (1) Phosphatidile choline
Explanation
Phosphatidylcholine is a phospholipid, which is a major structural component of the Milk Fat Globule Membrane (MFGM), not a water-soluble whey protein.

Q14
Enzyme that causes hydrolysis of lactose in cheese whey is
(1) Glucose oxidase
(2) 8-galactosidase
(3) Lipase
(4) a-amylase
Answer: (2) 8-galactosidase
Explanation
β-galactosidase (lactase) is the specific enzyme that hydrolyzes the disaccharide lactose into its constituent monosaccharides: glucose and galactose.
Q15
Statement I: Bactofugation removes bacterial cells/spores. Statement II: Prevents late blowing, weakens coagulum, overcome by adding CaCl2.
(1) Both true
(2) Both false
(3) I is true, II is false
(4) I is false, II is true
Answer: (1) Both true
Explanation
Bactofugation efficiently removes Clostridium spores (preventing late blowing). However, the high-speed centrifugal action slightly damages the protein structure, weakening the curd, which is counteracted by adding Calcium Chloride.
Q16
Problems associated with Buffalo milk Cheddar cheese: A. Faster renneting and lower retention of moisture. B. Faster acidity development. C. Slower proteolysis/lipolysis. D. Hard, rubbery texture.
(1) A, B and C only
(2) A, C and D only
(3) B, C and D only
(4) A, B and D only
Answer: (2) A, C and D only
Explanation
Buffalo milk has a higher buffering capacity, resulting in SLOWER acidity development, making statement B false. It does have faster renneting, slower ripening, and tends to form a hard, rubbery texture.
Q17
Which of the following membrane technology allows retention of lactose, protein, fat and minerals?
(1) Reverse Osmosis
(2) Ultrafiltration
(3) Diafiltration
(4) Microfiltration
Answer: (1) Reverse Osmosis
Explanation
Reverse Osmosis (RO) has the tightest pore size, allowing practically only pure water to pass through. It perfectly retains lactose, proteins, fat, and dissolved minerals.
Q18

(1) 1 nm

Explanation
Reverse Osmosis membranes possess microscopic pores estimated to be in the range of 0.1 to 1 nanometer (nm), with ~0.2 nm being a standard operational threshold to reject salts and sugars.
Q19
SSHE type heat exchanger could be used in the continuous production of
(1) Rasogolla
(2) Paneer
(3) Khoa
(4) Sandesh
Answer: (3) Khoa
Explanation
A Scraped Surface Heat Exchanger (SSHE) is specifically engineered to handle highly viscous, sticky materials that foul easily, making it the perfect continuous industrial replacement for the traditional open-pan boiling of Khoa.
Q20
Which of the following is/are reason(s) of heat coagulation of milk during khoa production? A. Altered salt balance. B. Decrease in pH. C. Increase in total solids. D. High temperature. E. Continuous stirring.
(1) B and E only
(2) B, C and D only
(3) A, B, C and D only
(4) B only
Answer: (3) A, B, C and D only
Explanation
Khoa formation is caused by the combined forces of massive heat (D), concentration of solids (C), the resulting drop in pH (B), and the destabilization of the mineral salt balance (A). Stirring prevents burning but does not *cause* coagulation.
Q21
The frying temperature of gulab jamun balls during industrial scale production is
(1) 140°C
(2) 132°C
(3) 148°C
(4) 128°C
Answer: (1) 140°C
Explanation
For uniform expansion, cooking of the core, and proper Maillard browning without burning the exterior, industrial Gulab Jamun frying is strictly controlled around 140°C to 145°C.
Q22
Assertion A: Milk is heated to high temp (90-95°C) prior to coagulation in paneer. Reason R: Imparts cooked flavour, enhances shelf life, rapid isoelectric precipitation, good body/texture.
(1) Both A and R correct, R is correct exp
(2) Both A and R correct, R is NOT correct exp
(3) A is correct but R is not
(4) A is not correct but R is correct
Answer: (1) Both A and R correct, R is correct exp
Explanation
Heating to 90°C denatures whey proteins (specifically β-lactoglobulin), which then co-precipitate with casein. This massive co-precipitation is what creates the firm, spongy, characteristic body of Paneer.

It typically takes about 2 to 2.5 grams of dry citric acid to coagulate 1 liter of milk. Using a 1% solution (1g/100ml), this requires roughly 200 ml of the coagulant solution.
Q25

(1) 23 microns
(2) 12 microns
(3) 15 microns
(4) 29 microns
Answer: (3) 15 microns
Explanation
The human tongue detects crystals as 'sandy' or gritty when they exceed 15 to 20 microns. Therefore, forced crystallization must keep the longest edge below 15 microns.

Explanation
The 'cooked' flavor stems from volatile sulfhydryl groups released by the denaturation of β-lactoglobulin, which begins perceptibly right around 76°C to 78°C.

A 'short' body in butter means it is brittle, lacks cohesion, snaps when bent, and completely lacks the desired smooth, waxy plasticity.

Q29
Which of the following microorganisms is responsible for gassy body in sweetened condensed milk?
(1) Aspergillus glaucus
(2) Escherichia coli
(3) Torula lactis condensi
(4) Streptococcus lactic subsp. Maltigens
Answer: (3) Torula lactis condensi
Explanation
Because SCM has a very high osmotic pressure (aw ~0.83), regular bacteria like E. coli cannot survive. Only osmophilic yeasts, specifically *Torula lactis- condensi*, can ferment the sucrose and blow the can.
Q30
Which of the following is a characteristic of Leaky butter?
(1) Dry appearance that readily falls apart
(2) Presence of moisture in the sampling device
(3) Rugged surface of butter
(4) Presence of coloured specks
Answer: (2) Presence of moisture in the sampling device
Explanation
Leaky butter indicates that the water droplets are too large and not properly emulsified. Upon plugging or cutting, visible drops of moisture literally 'leak' out onto the sampling trier.
Q31
Which of the following is NOT responsible for flat flavour in Cheddar cheese?
(1) Low curing temperature
(2) Short ripening time
(3) Use of low fat milk
(4) Low cooking temperature
Answer: (4) Low cooking temperature
Explanation
Flat flavor means a lack of developed cheese taste. This is caused by low fat (flavor solvent), short ripening (no breakdown), or low curing temp (inactive enzymes). Low cooking temperature actually leaves excess moisture, promoting sour/ bitter, not flat flavors.
Q32
Which among the following is NOT a physical sense?
(1) Hearing
(2) Touch
(3) Smell
(4) Temperature
Answer: (3) Smell
Explanation
Hearing, touch, and temperature are physical/mechanical senses. Smell (olfaction) and taste (gustation) are fundamentally *Chemical* senses, relying on molecular receptor binding.
Q33

Explanation
Milk Protein Concentrate (MPC) is produced strictly via Ultrafiltration, which concentrates both casein and whey proteins without subjecting them to the severe heat denaturation seen in co-precipitates.
Q34

Q35

Explanation
WPNI, Casein number, and Cysteine number all relate to protein denaturation levels. Urea number is a metric of milk adulteration or animal feed efficiency, having zero relation to powder heat classification.

The 'Re-wet' (or Agglomeration) process takes fine milk powder, lightly wets the surfaces with steam to make them sticky, and collides them into larger, porous clusters that instantly sink and dissolve in water.

Q38

Q39

(3) Lactoperoxidase test

Answer: (1) Farrall index
Explanation
The Farrall Index is the standard microscopic evaluation used to assess homogenization efficiency by physically counting and sizing the remaining large fat globules in a sample.
Q40
The 'foam theory of churning' has been put forth by following scientist(s):
(1) Rahn
(2) King
(3) Fisher and Hooker
(4) Van Dam and Holwerda
Answer: (1) Rahn
Explanation
Otto Rahn (1928) proposed the Foam Theory, stating that during churning, milk fat globules gather at the air-water interface of air bubbles, where the membrane is sheared off, causing the fat to clump when the bubble collapses.

Q42

Q43

Q44

Explanation
Mottling (uneven, blotchy coloring) is caused when large, coarse salt crystals fail to dissolve evenly in the butter moisture, drawing water towards them and creating pale, localized spots.

Explanation
A two-stage homogenizer features exactly two pressure gauges: one indicating the massive first-stage pressure (e.g., 2000 psi) and one indicating the lower second-stage back-pressure (e.g., 500 psi).
Q46

While clarifiers use centrifugal force to easily eject heavy dirt, somatic cells (leucocytes), and dense debris, they operate at forces too low to effectively remove the vast majority of microscopic, low-density bacteria.

Q49

Q50
