Cream Separator

A detailed dairy-engineering guide to centrifugal separation of milk into cream and skim milk — covering the separation mechanism, disc bowl, components, operating temperature, outlet systems, skimming efficiency and maintenance.

WHAT THE EQUIPMENT DOES

Milk → Cream + Skim Milk

A cream separator is a high-speed centrifugal separator used to recover milk fat as cream while producing a low-fat skim-milk stream. Separation is driven mainly by the density difference between fat globules and the continuous skim-milk phase.

Whole milk feed→Rotating bowl→Cream+Skim milk
Separation direction: the lower-density fat globules migrate inward toward the axis of rotation, while the denser skim-milk phase migrates outward toward the bowl periphery.

01 · What Is a Cream Separator?

A cream separator is a centrifugal machine designed to separate milk into a fat-rich cream phase and a skim-milk phase. Milk is a dispersion in which fat globules are less dense than the aqueous skim-milk phase.

When milk enters a rapidly rotating bowl, the centrifugal field causes the denser material to migrate toward the bowl wall and the lighter fat globules to migrate toward the axis. A disc stack provides many closely spaced separation channels, increasing the effective separation area.

Cream — light phase

Fat globules migrate inward toward the axis of rotation and are collected through the cream outlet.

Skim milk — heavy phase

The denser continuous phase migrates outward toward the bowl periphery and is discharged through the skim-milk outlet.

02 · Working Principle — Step by Step

1

Milk enters the separator

Warm milk enters the bowl through the feed and distribution system and is accelerated to the rotational speed of the bowl.

2

Centrifugal field is created

The rapidly rotating bowl generates a centrifugal field. The effective force is many times greater than gravity, so density differences cause rapid phase migration.

3

Disc stack divides the flow

Milk passes through numerous narrow channels formed by the closely spaced conical discs. The short separation distance improves the opportunity for fat globules to migrate.

4

Fat globules move inward

Because fat is less dense than skim milk, fat globules migrate toward the axis. They coalesce into the cream phase.

5

Skim milk moves outward

The denser skim-milk phase moves toward the bowl periphery. Dense sediment and foreign matter also tend to accumulate in the outer sediment region.

6

Separate outlets recover the phases

Cream is discharged from the inner region and skim milk from the outer region. Outlet systems can use paring discs or hermetic/centripetal arrangements.

04 · Main Components

Separator bowl assembly

  • Bowl body: rotating lower section forming the main separation chamber.
  • Bowl hood: upper cover that closes and shapes the flow path.
  • Lock ring: secures the bowl body and hood.
  • Distributor: receives incoming milk and distributes it toward the disc stack.
  • Disc stack: closely spaced conical discs where separation occurs.
  • Distribution holes: aligned openings through which milk enters the disc channels.
  • Sediment space: peripheral region where dense impurities accumulate.

Drive & outlet system

  • Spindle: carries and rotates the bowl.
  • Gear / drive: transmits motor power to the high-speed spindle.
  • Motor & clutch: provide and transmit drive power.
  • Cream outlet: removes the light phase.
  • Skim-milk outlet: removes the heavy continuous phase.
  • Paring discs / outlet pumps: recover rotating liquid and convert kinetic energy into discharge pressure.
  • Operating-water system: used in self-cleaning/sludge-ejecting bowl designs.

05 · Disc Stack — Why It Matters

The disc stack divides the milk into many thin channels. This reduces the distance through which fat globules need to migrate before reaching the cream side of the separation zone.

  • Many closely spaced discs provide a large effective separation area.
  • The inclined channels support controlled movement of the two phases.
  • Distribution holes help feed milk uniformly into the disc channels.
  • Disc condition, spacing and cleanliness directly affect performance.

06 · Why Around 45°C Is Suitable

1. Lower viscosity

As milk warms, its viscosity decreases. Lower viscosity reduces resistance to movement of fat globules through the liquid.

2. Better fat-globule movement

Warm milk allows fat globules to migrate more readily through the narrow disc channels under the centrifugal field.

3. Practical separation range

Around 40–45°C provides a useful warm-separation condition without unnecessarily increasing the thermal load on milk.

Why not keep increasing temperature? Higher temperature is not automatically better. Excessive heating can affect milk quality and, depending on equipment/materials, may reduce the service life of rubber components and packing. Use the validated separator operating condition.

07 · Open, Semi-open and Hermetic Designs

Open type

Milk is handled with greater exposure to atmospheric conditions. These machines are generally simpler and suited to smaller-capacity applications.

Semi-open / paring disc

Stationary paring devices recover the rotating liquid and convert part of its kinetic energy into outlet pressure.

Hermetic

The bowl remains filled with product and inlet/outlet connections are arranged for a closed process, minimizing air contact.

08 · Outlet Systems

Paring-disc outlet

  • The stationary rim dips into the rotating liquid column.
  • Rotating liquid enters the paring-disc channels.
  • Kinetic energy is converted into pressure.
  • Downstream pressure influences outlet performance.

Hermetic / centripetal outlet

  • The bowl is completely filled with milk during operation.
  • Product enters through the hollow bowl spindle and distribution holes.
  • Outlet pumps discharge the separated phases without exposing product to an open air column.
  • Suitable for closed hygienic process piping.

09 · Skimming Efficiency — Calculation

Skimming efficiency expresses the fraction of the fat entering the separator that is recovered in the cream rather than remaining in the skim milk.

Fat entering separator = Qm × Fm / 100
Fat leaving with skim = Qs × Fs / 100
Fat recovered in cream = Fat entering − Fat in skim
η = [(QmFm − QsFs) / (QmFm)] × 100

Worked example

Milk flow = 10,000 L/h; milk fat = 4%; skim-milk flow = 9,000 L/h; skim fat = 0.05%.

Fat entering = 10,000 × 4 / 100 = 400 kg/h
Fat in skim = 9,000 × 0.05 / 100 = 4.5 kg/h
Fat recovered = 400 − 4.5 = 395.5 kg/h
η = (395.5 / 400) × 100 = 98.88%

Interpretation

  • Lower residual fat in skim milk generally indicates better skimming.
  • Efficiency is affected by throughput, temperature, bowl speed, disc condition, fat-globule characteristics and air entrainment.
  • Use consistent flow and fat-basis units in the calculation.

10 · Solids Removal & Self-cleaning Bowl

Besides fat separation, dense impurities move toward the bowl periphery and accumulate in the sediment space. Modern self-cleaning or solids-ejecting bowls can discharge accumulated sediment without frequent manual dismantling.

Typical sediment

Dense foreign matter and cellular material can collect in the sediment space. The exact material depends on milk quality and plant conditions.

Operating-water system

In self-cleaning designs, hydraulic operating water controls the sliding bowl bottom. A controlled opening releases accumulated solids and the bowl then closes again.

11 · Maintenance & Safety

  • Replace worn gaskets and O-rings before leakage or hygiene problems develop.
  • Maintain correct lubrication oil quality and level where applicable.
  • Inspect spindle and drive bearings for wear.
  • Clean discs carefully without damaging their surfaces.
  • Assemble the bowl and disc stack correctly to prevent vibration and loss of capacity.
  • Never run the separator dry when the equipment requires product/liquid for operation.
  • Never assemble, disassemble or adjust a rotating separator.
  • Verify correct spindle direction after motor maintenance.
  • Feed milk at the pressure specified for the particular separator.
  • Do not allow process-piping loads to strain separator connections.
